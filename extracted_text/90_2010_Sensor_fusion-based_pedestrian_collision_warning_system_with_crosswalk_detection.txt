Abstract—This paper describes a pedestrian collision 
warning system with crosswalk detection feature based 
on sensor fusion of a monocular camera and a millimeter 
wave radar. The method to decide about the presence of a 
pedestrian is based on the assumption that objects 
moving along a crosswalk can be interpreted as 
pedestrians under certain circumstances. The advantage 
of the described solution is its robustness and 
effectiveness since it is limited to crosswalks. The camera 
can be used to detect the crosswalk. Data from both 
sensors can then be used to infer about the presence of a 
pedestrian. The sensor fusion algorithm which combines 
data from the sensors is explained. Then the paper 
describes a warning concept which provides auditory 
alarm and visual information about the presence of a 
crosswalk as well as pedestrians to a driver, depending on 
the estimated collision probability. Finally, test drives on 
an experimental vehicle are presented and the results 
verify that the proposed warning system is running in 
practice. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
ccording to statistical data of accidents, pedestrian 
fatalities are a particular priority in Europe and Japan, as 
shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2 [1-2]. Recently, 
researches on driving assistance systems which are method of 
technical solutions for accident prevention have been 
conducted extensively [3]. To detect pedestrians, existing 
researches make use of stereo camera, monocular camera, 
infrared camera and LIDARs as described in the survey by 
Gandhi and Trivedi [4]. Cameras have advantages of wide 
view range and high resolution power, so cameras can 
recognize attributes of objects by image processing. However 
there are some problems of image processing that, the 
algorithm is so complex, the computational cost is high, and 
the distance to target might be inaccurate. On the other hand, 
a LIDAR is not suitable to recognize attributes of objects, but 
it can measure the distance accurately. For development of

II. EXPERIMENTAL VEHICLE  
The experimental vehicle used in this study was equipped 
with a millimeter wave radar, a monocular CMOS camera, 
PC, and a sensor cluster which gets the information of vehicle 
speed and yaw rate. The millimeter wave radar and the 
camera used in the study are Continental ARS300 and 
CSF200 respectively. The sensors and the measurement 
system configuration on the experimental vehicle are shown 
in Figure 3. 
 The 77GHz millimeter wave radar has two ranges. One is a 
long distance scanning range which has a measurement angle 
of 18 degree, and a detection range of 200 meters. The other 
is a short distance scanning range which has a measurement 
angle of 56 degree, and a detection range of 60 meters. On 
this sensor an algorithm for object detection and tracking is 
implemented. This algorithm segments feature points which 
are recognized by millimeter wave radar, and outputs the 
position data by tracking with Kalman filter. Thus, the 
position and the relative velocity of detected objects can be 
obtained by the millimeter wave radar. These data are input 
into a PC via a CAN-bus device and can be monitored in real 
time. The image from the monocular camera is captured by a 
measurement interface in real-time and the image is then 
delivered into the PC for the image processing task. The field 
of view of the camera is 42 deg (H) x 26 deg (V) with the 
resolution of 752 x 480 pixels, and its update rate is 40 
milliseconds.

III. PEDESTRIAN DETECTION AND WARNING SYSTEM DESIGN  
The architecture of the pedestrian collision warning system 
is shown in Figure 4. This system consists of three 
algorithms: a crosswalk detection algorithm, a sensor fusion 
algorithm of camera and millimeter wave radar, and an 
algorithm for calculation of the probability of pedestrian 
collision. A warning is provided to the driver when the 
pedestrian collision reaches a certain level.

C. Warning algorithm 
This algorithm provides a visual and an auditory warning 
in real time to a driver depending on a collision risk to the 
pedestrian. The warning algorithm estimates the collision risk 
from the predicted position of the pedestrian, and the host 
vehicle velocity and the crosswalk information explained in 
the previous section. Conventional warning system proposed 
in [5] used a simple index such as Time-to-Collision (TTC) as 
a criterion whether the warning should be issued or not. 
However, TTC is the index which considers the relative 
speed between two vehicles in identical directions, so TTC 
may not be suitable to indicate the risk when the object 
approaches to the host vehicle in lateral direction. Until now, 
the systems based on the current pedestrian longitudinal 
position have been proposed to estimate the collision risk [6]. 
Therefore, the systems based on TTC index might give 
inappropriate warning to drivers since they  do not  take the 
lateral position of the pedestrian into account. Therefore, this 
research presents the collision risk estimation which 
combines TTC with the prediction of pedestrian position for

IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 
Experiments using the test vehicle were conducted to 
confirm whether the proposed pedestrian collision avoidance 
system can work in principle. The vehicle was running at the 
constant speed of 20 km/h in perpendicular to a crosswalk. As 
shown in Figure 14, the experimental condition was that a 
pedestrian starts walking on the crosswalk if the 
time-to-collision 
tc
T is under 3.0 seconds and that the test 
vehicle does not slow down. We replaced a real pedestrian 
with puppet to secure the safety of the experiment. The 
experimental result is indicated in Figure 15 and 16. When 
the pedestrian entered the region of interest, the artificial 
traffic light on the display was changed from yellow to red. 
The trajectory of puppet with respect to the host vehicle 
position during the experiment is shown in Figure 17. 
According to Figure 17, the trajectory of puppet entered the 
region of interest (warning region). Therefore, the validity of 
the proposed pedestrian detection and warning system 
algorithm was verified.

V. CONCLUSIONS 
This paper described an algorithm for implementing a 
pedestrian detection and collision warning system for an 
experimental vehicle, limited to locations nearby crosswalks. 
The described system is promising since it is also easy to 
understand for the driver. The design principle used here to 
handle the pedestrian detection problem is based on the 
assumption that objects moving along a crosswalk can be 
inferred as pedestrians under certain circumstances. As a 
method for pedestrian collision prevention, visual and 
auditory warning systems are issued to drivers in real time 
based on the pedestrian position and movement, with respect 
to the host vehicle motion. The sensor setup on the 
experimental vehicle for pedestrian detection was presented 
and experimental results reveal that the proposed sensor 
fusion approach for pedestrian collision warning system can 
work in practice. 
Further investigations in public road driving will be 
conducted by using the same experimental setup indicated in 
this paper, and the evaluation of false alarm rates and missing 
detections in real-world driving will be conducted.
